## THE 17-POINT IMAGE SPECIFICATION FRAMEWORK

Every significant image project must be fully specified across these 17 dimensions before any prompt is written.

### 01. VISUAL OBJECTIVE
State what this image must accomplish. Not what it depicts — what it achieves. Example: "This image must create immediate desire for the product by making it look simultaneously premium and accessible."

### 02. MAIN SUBJECT
Define the primary subject with precision:
- Subject type (person, object, environment, abstraction)
- Subject identity (specific character, product model, location)
- Subject state (active, passive, in motion, at rest)
- Subject relationships (alone, with others, in context)
- Subject emotion or expression (if applicable)
- Subject physical characteristics (age, build, style, condition)

### 03. ENVIRONMENT & SETTING
Define where this takes place:
- Interior vs. exterior
- Specific location type (urban street, forest, studio, domestic, industrial)
- Time of day and resulting light quality
- Season and weather conditions
- Level of environmental detail (clean and minimal vs. richly detailed)
- Environmental mood and atmosphere

### 04. COMPOSITION
Specify the structural organization:
- Primary compositional rule (thirds, golden ratio, centered, dynamic symmetry)
- Focal point placement (where the eye goes first)
- Secondary focal points
- Positive vs. negative space balance
- Foreground, midground, background relationship
- Direction of implied movement or tension
- Frame edges treatment (breathing room vs. tight crop)

### 05. CAMERA PERSPECTIVE
Define the viewing angle:
- Eye level, low angle, high angle, overhead (bird's eye), ground level
- Distance from subject (extreme close-up, close-up, medium, medium-wide, wide, extreme wide)
- Degree of tilt or Dutch angle (if any)
- Perspective distortion level (telephoto compression vs. wide-angle expansion)

### 06. LENS CHARACTERISTICS
Specify the optical properties:
- Focal length equivalent (24mm, 35mm, 50mm, 85mm, 135mm, 200mm)
- Aperture effect (depth of field — sharp throughout vs. selective focus)
- Bokeh quality (if applicable — smooth circles, swirly, hexagonal)
- Distortion (barrel distortion for wide, compression for telephoto)
- Vignetting (slight falloff at edges vs. clean flat field)

### 07. LIGHTING SETUP
Define the complete lighting environment:
- Primary light source (sun direction, studio strobe, natural window, practical lamp)
- Key light quality (hard vs. soft)
- Key light direction (front, 45°, side, back, rim)
- Fill light level (high fill = low contrast; low fill = high contrast)
- Rim/hair light presence (yes/no, strength)
- Background light treatment
- Color temperature (warm/cool/mixed)
- Atmospheric effects (fog, haze, dust, god rays, lens flare — when motivated)
- Shadow behavior (sharp/soft, length, color)

### 08. COLOR GRADING
Define the color treatment:
- Overall palette (dominant color, supporting colors, accent color)
- Color temperature of the grade
- Saturation level (desaturated/muted, natural, vibrant, hyper-real)
- Contrast (low contrast/flat, standard, high contrast)
- Specific color relationships (shadows pushed blue, highlights pushed warm, etc.)
- Reference visual or color grade name (if applicable)
- Film emulation (if appropriate — Kodak Portra, Fuji Velvia, etc.)

### 09. MATERIALS & TEXTURES
Define the tactile qualities of surfaces:
- Subject material (what it is made of, its surface quality)
- Environmental materials
- Finish quality (matte, satin, gloss, metallic)
- Texture level (smooth/minimal to rich/detailed)
- Weathering or aging (new, used, aged, distressed)

### 10. DEPTH & ATMOSPHERE
Define the three-dimensional quality:
- Depth of field range (deep focus vs. razor-thin)
- Atmospheric haze level
- Distance cues (how far does the scene extend)
- Atmospheric particles (dust, fog, smoke, rain)
- Air quality (clear, hazy, smoky, misty)

### 11. BACKGROUND TREATMENT
Specify the background precisely:
- Pure studio (infinite white, gray, black, color)
- Environmental (location-specific, detailed)
- Abstracted environment (location implied, not detailed)
- Textured (surface only, no specific location)
- Out-of-focus environmental (bokeh background)

### 12. VISUAL HIERARCHY
Confirm the attention structure:
- What commands attention first?
- What commands attention second?
- What is secondary/supporting?
- What should recede or be ignored?
- Are there any competing focal points that need to be resolved?

### 13. ASPECT RATIO
Specify format:
- 1:1 (square — Instagram, profile)
- 4:5 (portrait social — Instagram feed)
- 9:16 (stories/reels — vertical video format)
- 16:9 (standard landscape — YouTube, desktop)
- 3:2 (photography standard — 35mm)
- 2:3 (portrait photography)
- 21:9 (cinematic ultra-wide)
- A4/A3 (print)
- Custom dimensions with specific pixel dimensions

### 14. RESOLUTION REQUIREMENTS
Define technical output:
- Web: 72 DPI, minimum 1920px wide
- Social: 72 DPI, platform-specific (see `15-technical-standards.md`)
- Print: 300 DPI at final print size
- Large format: 150 DPI at final size
- Specific pixel dimensions when known

### 15. STYLE & AESTHETIC REFERENCE
Define the visual language:
- Photographic style (documentary, editorial, advertising, fine art, vernacular)
- Era or historical reference (1970s photography, Art Deco, Bauhaus, etc.)
- Artist or director reference (style of, not copy of)
- Mood board description (what images would be in the reference collection)
- What to avoid (what styles would be wrong for this)

### 16. NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
Define what must not appear:
- Specific visual elements to exclude
- Aesthetic styles to avoid
- Technical artifacts to prevent (blur, noise, distortion, artifacts)
- Content restrictions
- Brand conflicts

### 17. QUALITY EVALUATION CRITERIA
Define how success will be measured:
- What must the viewer feel?
- What must be clearly communicated?
- What technical standards must be met?
- What would cause rejection?

## DISCIPLINE-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE

### Product Photography

**The fundamental rule**: The product is the subject. Everything else serves the product.

**Common mistakes**:
- Lighting that obscures product details
- Background that competes with product color
- Angle that distorts product proportions
- Styling props that overwhelm the product
- Over-retouching that makes the product look fake

**Key considerations**:
- Show the product's most distinctive angle, not necessarily its front
- Demonstrate scale (props, context, or dimensions)
- Reveal material quality through light interaction
- Show the product in use when relevant
- Establish brand positioning through the surrounding environment and styling

### Food Photography

**The fundamental rule**: Food must look delicious, not perfect.

**Common mistakes**:
- Over-styled food that looks artificial
- Lighting too flat (no depth or texture revealed)
- Steam/condensation effects that look fake
- Garnishes that obscure the food
- Background too busy

**Key considerations**:
- Use hard side lighting to reveal texture
- Steam must be real (or composited convincingly)
- Color saturation of food slightly increased adds appetite appeal
- Imperfections (a crumb, a drip, a slight lean) add authenticity
- The story of preparation adds value (ingredients nearby, tools visible)

### Character Design / Concept Art

**The fundamental rule**: Character must feel like they exist in a world.

**Common mistakes**:
- Perfect symmetry (humans are not symmetrical)
- Too-clean lighting that feels ungrounded
- No environmental context
- Generic fantasy/sci-fi design tropes without creative thinking
- Clothing and equipment that have no visual logic or story

**Key considerations**:
- Design from the character's story outward
- Every piece of costume/equipment should have a reason
- The face should contain history and experience
- Silhouette must be readable (test at small size)
- Design must work in motion, not just static pose

### Architecture Visualization

**The fundamental rule**: Show the experience of the space, not just its geometry.

**Common mistakes**:
- Lighting that reveals structure but no atmosphere
- Empty spaces with no suggestion of inhabitation
- Views that show technical achievement but not livability
- Over-saturated skies that compete with the architecture

**Key considerations**:
- Include people (even as small figures) to establish scale and life
- Choose the time of day that shows the design at its best
- Use environmental context (landscape, street, sky) as compositional elements
- Show materials in their best light — literally
- Consider the architect's intention and honor it

## TEXT IN IMAGES

AI image generators have inconsistent text rendering capability. RENDER BEAST provides two strategies:

### Strategy 1 — Generator-Dependent (when text matters less)
Include text as part of the prompt but accept imperfect rendering. Only viable for decorative or background text. Never for primary communication.

### Strategy 2 — Post-Production Typography (recommended for text-critical work)
1. Generate the image without text (describe the composition as if text were present but generate the visual layer only)
2. Provide a separate typography specification: font, size, weight, color, position, tracking, alignment
3. Composite text in post-production (Figma, Photoshop, Canva, or similar)
4. This produces professional typographic quality that generators cannot match

Always use Strategy 2 when: text is a headline, tagline, logo, price, date, or any primary communication element.

## POST-PRODUCTION INSTRUCTIONS

For every image deliverable, include applicable post-production guidance:

**Color Corrections**
- White balance adjustment needed (Y/N)
- Exposure compensation direction (brighter/darker)
- Contrast adjustment (increase/decrease)
- Specific color channel work needed

**Retouching**
- Subject retouching level (none/light/heavy)
- Background cleanup requirements
- Object removal or addition needs
- Skin retouching philosophy (natural/editorial)

**Compositing**
- Background replacement requirements
- Foreground/background separation requirements
- Shadow creation for composited elements
- Color matching between composited layers

**Typography Overlay**
- Text to be added (see Strategy 2 above)
- Font specifications
- Color and contrast requirements
- Size and positioning

**Export**
- Output format (see `15-technical-standards.md`)
- Delivery size
- Color profile
- Naming convention

## IMAGE BEAST QUALITY RULES

The following are strictly prohibited in final IMAGE BEAST deliverables:

- Accidental visual clutter (elements that were not specified)
- Meaningless decorative elements
- Inconsistent anatomical structure
- Random object placement without compositional logic
- Inconsistent perspective between elements
- Malformed or twisted geometry
- Shadows that do not match the light source
- Incoherent lighting (light coming from multiple inconsistent directions)
- Unnecessary text or watermarks
- Generic stock-photo composition (everyone smiling at camera, businessman shaking hands)
- Repetitive AI aesthetic (over-smooth skin, over-perfect symmetry, generic cinematic grade)
- Text that is unreadable or misspelled
- Extreme JPEG or generation artifacts
- Color banding or posterization
