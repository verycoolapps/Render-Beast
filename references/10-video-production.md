## OVERVIEW

CINEMA BEAST approaches every video project as a professional film and motion production studio. A video is not a collection of attractive shots — it is a sequence with intention, structure, emotional progression, and narrative coherence.

## PRE-PRODUCTION FRAMEWORK

Every video project begins with a complete pre-production package before any shot is described.

### 1. CREATIVE BRIEF

Define:
- Project name and type
- Brand or creator
- Deliverable specs (duration, format, platform)
- Deadline considerations
- Budget implications on production scale

### 2. CENTRAL CONCEPT

The concept is one clear, compelling sentence that contains the entire creative vision. It is not a description of what happens — it is a statement of what the video means.

**Wrong**: "A product commercial showing features of our running shoe."
**Right**: "A film about the specific kind of silence that follows the moment a runner decides to push further than they thought possible."

The concept governs every creative decision: what we show, how we show it, what music we use, how we cut, what we do not show.

### 3. STORYLINE & STRUCTURE

Define the emotional journey:

**Three-Act Structure (for narrative work)**
- Act 1 (Setup): Establish world, character, or situation. Create stakes.
- Act 2 (Conflict/Development): Introduce tension, complexity, or escalation.
- Act 3 (Resolution): Deliver the payoff, the reveal, the emotional peak.

**Commercial Structure Options**:
- Problem → Solution → Brand
- Situation → Emotion → Brand (no problem shown)
- Product Feature → Benefit → Proof
- Brand World → Story → Call to Action
- Emotional Statement → Visual Proof → Brand

**Social Content Structure**:
- Hook (0-2 seconds: must stop the scroll)
- Core content (the value, the story, the reveal)
- Resolution (the payoff that makes watching worthwhile)
- CTA (what to do next)

### 4. AUDIENCE & PLATFORM

Specify:
- Primary audience (demographics, psychographics)
- Platform (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, broadcast, cinema, internal)
- Viewing context (mobile/small screen in noise, desktop with sound, cinema with full attention)
- Platform-specific requirements (safe zones, aspect ratios, caption requirements)

### 5. EMOTIONAL PROGRESSION

Map the emotional journey beat by beat:
- Opening emotion (how the viewer feels at second 0)
- Inflection points (when emotion shifts and in what direction)
- Peak emotion (the highest intensity moment)
- Closing emotion (how the viewer feels when it ends)
- Desired carry-away emotion (what they feel 5 minutes later)

### 6. VISUAL TREATMENT DOCUMENT

Establish the complete visual language:
- Cinematography style (documentary, advertising, editorial, experimental)
- Color palette and grading direction
- Lighting style and quality
- Camera movement language
- Editing rhythm and style
- Typography style and motion
- Music genre and energy
- Sound design philosophy

## CINEMATOGRAPHY SYSTEM

### Shot Sizes

| Name | Abbreviation | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme Close-Up | ECU | Detail of face, hands, object |
| Close-Up | CU | Head and shoulders, specific object |
| Medium Close-Up | MCU | Chest up — most interview standard |
| Medium Shot | MS | Waist up — natural conversational |
| Medium Wide | MW | Knees up — shows context |
| Wide Shot | WS | Full figure with environment |
| Extreme Wide | EWS | Environment dominant, figure small |

### Camera Angles

**Eye Level** — Neutral, relatable, honest. The default.
**Low Angle** — Subject looks powerful, monumental, or threatening.
**High Angle** — Subject looks vulnerable, small, or watched.
**Dutch Angle (Canted)** — Disorientation, unease, stylistic.
**Bird's Eye (Overhead)** — Pattern, scale, surveillance, design.
**Worm's Eye** — Extreme low angle. Extreme power dynamic.

### Camera Movements

**Static** — No camera movement. Stability, gravity, observation.
**Pan** — Horizontal rotation. Following action, revealing environment.
**Tilt** — Vertical rotation. Revealing height, following vertical movement.
**Dolly/Track** — Camera moves through space. Immersion, following.
**Push In / Pull Out** — Closing distance / opening distance. Intensity / revelation.
**Crane/Jib** — Vertical movement with extended reach. Scale, drama.
**Handheld** — Camera follows human breathing. Energy, authenticity, unease.
**Steadicam** — Smooth handheld movement. Following, immersion without shake.
**Drone** — Aerial movement. Scale, geography, spectacle.
**Rack Focus** — Shifting focus between subjects. Directing attention, connection.
**Whip Pan** — Fast pan creating blur. Speed, energy, transition.
**Dutch Tilt Roll** — Rotating around lens axis. Disorientation.

### Lens Selection

**Wide (10-24mm)**: Environmental, immersive, slightly distorted. Use for establishing shots, interiors, action.

**Standard (35-50mm)**: Natural perspective, closest to human eye. Use for most storytelling situations.

**Portrait (85-105mm)**: Beautiful compression, subject separation. Use for faces, character moments, products.

**Telephoto (135-300mm+)**: Strong compression, subject isolation, long reach. Use for action, wildlife, street, surveillance aesthetic.

### Depth of Field

**Deep Focus** (small aperture, wide lens): Everything sharp. Epic, environmental, documentation.
**Standard** (medium aperture): Subject sharp, background slightly soft. Natural.
**Shallow** (large aperture, long lens): Subject sharp, background bokeh. Cinematic, intimate, premium.
**Razor-thin** (maximum aperture): Extreme isolation. Fashion, luxury, macro, art.

```
SCENE 01 — THE WORLD
TIME: 00:00–00:03 | DURATION: 3s

VISUAL: Extreme wide shot of a mountain trail at golden hour. The landscape is massive. A tiny figure runs along the ridge.

CAMERA: EWS static on tripod. 200mm telephoto compressing the distance. Subject tiny in frame.

LIGHTING: Golden hour. Sun at 20° above horizon, slightly left. Long shadows. Warm 3000K atmosphere.

SUBJECT ACTION: Running steadily left-to-right along the ridge. Smooth, effortless.

CAMERA: MCU. 85mm. Shallow depth of field. Background (trail, sky) beautifully blurred. Slight handheld — breathing movement.

LIGHTING: Golden side light from camera-right. Rim light from behind adds separation. Slight fill from left sky.

SUBJECT ACTION: Running. Face forward. A small exhale. Eyes focused on something we cannot see.

EMOTIONAL BEAT: Determination. The internal experience.

TRANSITION OUT: Cut
```

## CONTINUITY RULES

Video continuity is maintained through consistent:

**Character Continuity**
- Clothing (same outfit unless time jump is signaled)
- Hair (same style and position)
- Accessories and props
- Makeup/skin condition
- Body language and posture consistency

**Environmental Continuity**
- Lighting direction (the sun stays on the same side)
- Color temperature (consistent grade)
- Environmental state (weather, time of day — unless transition)
- Object positions

**Camera Language Continuity**
- Consistent shooting style (handheld stays handheld)
- Consistent focal length relationships (do not go from 200mm to 24mm on same subject without motivation)
- Screen direction (subject moving left-to-right stays left-to-right unless crossing the line is intentional)

**Audio Continuity**
- Consistent room tone (the background sound of an environment)
- Consistent voice quality (no processing changes mid-interview)
- Music transitions (do not abruptly drop or change genre)

## SOUND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Sound is 50% of video. Never treat it as an afterthought.

**Diegetic Sound** — Sounds that exist within the story world (footsteps, ambient noise, dialogue)
**Non-Diegetic Sound** — Sounds that exist outside the story world (music, narration, sound effects added in post)

**Sound Design Brief Template**:
- Ambient environment (what the world sounds like)
- Subject-specific sounds (what their actions produce)
- Emotional audio punctuation (sounds that emphasize emotional moments)
- Music relationship (does it dominate, support, or contrast the visual?)
- Silence (when is the absence of sound used?)

---

## MUSIC DIRECTION LANGUAGE

Provide specific, actionable music direction:

**Genre Terms**: orchestral, electronic, ambient, acoustic, jazz, folk, hip-hop, classical, hybrid (orchestral + electronic)

**Energy Terms**: intense, restrained, building, explosive, meditative, urgent, serene, melancholic

**Tempo Terms**: slow (60-80 BPM), moderate (80-110 BPM), fast (110-140 BPM), very fast (140+)

**Structural Terms**: builds to climax at [timestamp], drops to minimal at [timestamp], swells when [action], resolves at [timestamp]

**Reference Terms**: "Similar in energy to [film score section]" — describe what you mean, never assume model knowledge of specific tracks

## CINEMA BEAST QUALITY RULES

The following are strictly prohibited:

- Random scene changes without narrative purpose
- Camera movement without a reason for the movement
- Objects introduced without being established
- Continuity breaks between scenes
- Sound design that ignores the visual environment
- Music that fights the emotional beat
- Text on screen that is unreadable or unnecessary
- Lighting that changes direction within the same scene
- Color grading that is inconsistent across cuts
- Fake "cinematic quality" (lens flares with no source, excessive grading with nothing to grade)
- Slow motion applied to footage that is not interesting at any speed
- Transitions (wipes, dissolves) used because they are available rather than because they communicate something
- Credits with generic animation
- Generic stock footage inserted to fill duration

## PROMPT GENERATION FOR CLIP-BASED TOOLS

When using text-to-video generators that produce short clips (Runway, Pika, Sora, etc.):

1. Generate one prompt per scene
2. Include only what can be shown in a single continuous clip
3. Specify exact camera movement (generators handle one movement per clip well; multiple movements rarely succeed)
4. Reference the continuity specification when crafting each prompt
5. Provide editing instructions for assembling clips

**Single-Shot Clip Prompt Format**:
```
[Subject + action + environment] [camera framing] [camera movement] [lighting] [style]

Example:
"Close-up shot of a runner's determined face, golden hour mountain light, slight handheld movement following their breathing rhythm, warm cinematic grade, shallow depth of field, photorealistic"
```
